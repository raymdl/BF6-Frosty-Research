Read this first

  1. A stored number is a setting, not a proven stat.The game files hold a huge number of values. We can read them exactly. We usually cannot say what unit they use or what they do in a match. A tank gun with a stored "900" is not shown to fire 900 shots a minute.
  2. Defaults can differ from what a weapon really uses.A registry default is a fallback value from a central table. An inherited default is a value a weapon picks up from a shared base type. A weapon's own value comes first, and even that may not be what plays out in a match. Example: the IFV missile stores its own damage as 250, while its registry default says 150.
  3. The strongest evidence is a description that matches a stored value.When the game's own text says one thing and the files hold the same number, the two agree and we trust the reading more. It still does not prove units or effects. For vehicles we have no clean match of this kind yet. The nearest is the UH-60 helicopter: its card says 5 seats and the files hold five seat entries. That counts entries, not usable seats. The companion Gadget guide has four clean matches.

How sure means two things. First, did we read the number correctly from the game files. That is almost always yes, and we re-read many of them from the raw files. Second, do we know what the number does in a match. That is usually no.

Loadout menu L3023, L3150, L3254

  • Game descriptionThe loadout menu has three numbered loadouts, "LOADOUT 1", "LOADOUT 2" and "LOADOUT 3", for each of five vehicle types: anti-air, attack helicopter, APC, IFV and MBT. That is 15 presets. Their text describes a role. It does not name equipment.

How sure The text and the stored picks are read correctly. Whether LOADOUT 1 is the default is not shown. It is only the first entry in the list.

Still unknown
  • Which loadout a player starts with.
  • Which cards belong to which vehicles.
  • Whether the seat counts on the cards match real usable seats.

Health and repair

Health and repair setting L3001, L3024, L3034, L3203, L3304

  • Stored game valueThe CB90 boat stores MaxHealth 1000 and RepairRateModifier 1. We read both straight from its own entry and re-read them from the raw file.
  • Stored game valueVehicle-level health stored for tanks: Leopard 1200, Bradley 1000, CV90 1000, AAV7A1 1000. The Abrams stores 1200 at the vehicle level, and each of its armour parts stores 1000. Wreck parts store 100.
  • Stored game valueEach tank has eight named parts: Front, Left, Right, Rear, two special parts and two wreck parts.
  • Inherited defaultFor 35 vehicle families we use the same health names as the CB90, because their files share the same layout. Only the CB90 is confirmed directly. The AAV7A1, The Beast and the Jet Ski use a different layout and get no health names.
  • Our guessA repair modifier of 1 probably means "no change", but we have not found the repair rate it modifies.

How sure Numbers: solid. Meaning: unproven. We do not know the unit of 1000 or how parts and the vehicle total combine.

Still unknown
  • How the stored health turns into hit points.
  • The repair rate, and what the modifier changes.
  • How part health and vehicle health work together.

Tank damage by direction

Players know that hitting a tank from behind hurts it most. The game files hold curves that fit that, but we have not found the formula.

Direction curves and damage numbers L3200, L3201, L3202, L3203, L3204, L3206, L3208

  • Stored game valueAngle curves exist for tanks and infantry fighting vehicles. Rear is above side, and side is above front. The tank rear curve is flat at 3.2 between 76 and 104 degrees and falls to 1.0 at the edges. Only the Abrams and CV90 have these curves attached in the files we read. The Leopard, Bradley and AAV7A1 have none attached.
  • EA patch notesEA update notes give damage ranges against tanks, front to rear: RPG-7V2 188 to 500, M136 AT 207 to 550, 120 mm tank shell 244 to 650, attack helicopter heavy rockets 120 to 320, IFV light rockets 75 to 200. Flat amounts: IFV lock-guided missile 450, C-4 350, MBT-LAW 240.
  • Stored game valueStored damage on the weapons themselves: RPG-7V2 135, M136 AT 125, MBT-LAW 100, IFV missile 250, tank shell 150 at the start and 135 at the end.
  • Inherited defaultRegistry defaults differ from those own values: RPG-7V2 default 125 against its own 135, tank shell default 130 against 150, IFV missile default 150 against 250. The own value is the one the weapon stores.
  • Our guessThe pattern rear over side over front fits both the EA numbers and the curves. The sizes do not line up. Every EA range runs from its smallest to its largest by a ratio of about 2.67. The rear curve is 3.2 times the glancing value. Dividing 3.2 by 1.2 gives 2.67, which looks like a match but has no known reason.

How sure The curves are read correctly. The link between curves, stored damage and the EA numbers is a guess. No formula is proven.

Still unknown
  • How the angle curve, part health and weapon damage combine.
  • Why the Abrams stores 1200 while the EA numbers assume a 1000-health tank.
  • Which side of the hit the angle is measured from.
  • Why a reported player test of side damage (375) is a little above what the curve gives (about 357).

Missile and shell numbers

Each projectile stores its own damage, blast and speed values. The tables show what is stored. None of it is proven as in-match damage.

Remote turret grenade launcher (Bradley and CV90) L3013, L3304

  • Stored game valueDirect hit damage 32 at the start and 32 at the end, between 15 and 350 in its falloff setting. Blast damage 70, inner blast radius 1, blast radius 4. Shockwave damage 1 with radius 7.5.
  • Stored game valueThe dud version of the shell stores blast damage 0, blast radius 5 and shockwave damage 0.
  • Stored game valueIts gun stores a fire-rate setting of 325, a magazine setting of 5 and a magazines setting of 10.

How sure Numbers: solid, re-read from the raw file. Meaning: units unknown, and no total damage is proven.

Still unknown
  • Whether direct plus blast add up to a total, and how.
  • The unit of the radii.
  • How the same shell behaves on other mounts that share it.

TOW2B and Hydra rockets L3300, L3302

  • Stored game valueTOW2B stores its own Damage 150, MaxSpeed 150 and Drag 0. Hydra70 stores Damage 15 and MaxSpeed 740.
  • Inherited defaultBoth also carry an inherited launch speed: 350 for the TOW2B and 250 for the Hydra. These do not agree with their own MaxSpeed. The inherited speed may not drive flight at all.
  • Inherited defaultThe air-vehicle TOW is a separate case. Its central table lists 120 and 20, while its own entry stores 90 and 0. The own entry is the one to read.

How sure Own values: solid. What the inherited values do: unknown.

Still unknown
  • Which speed the missile actually flies at.
  • The unit of every speed and time value.

  • Stored game valueMBT guided shell, stationary TOW and AA aim-guided missile each store nine own fields. The tables below show them.
  • Stored game valueTheir minimum-blast-damage fields store 0 on the MBT guided shell and the AA missile. That is a stored zero, not proof of no minimum damage.
  • Our guessGuidance method is stored as the number 3 on two of them. We have no readable name for 3.

Inherited launch speed and lifetime

Thirty vehicle missile and shell types store a launch speed and a lifetime picked up from a shared base type. They are inherited defaults. We have not shown them to be the real launch speed or lifetime. All values were re-read from the raw files on 2 October.

What the inherited pair looks like L3302

  • Inherited defaultLaunch speed 350 appears on 20 types: every air-guided missile, every ground-guided missile and bomb, the Hellfire and the Spike, plus the AA high-velocity missile and the vehicle Stinger. Lifetimes differ: 15 on most air-guided types, 60 on the bombs, 10 on Maverick and Hellfire, 6 on the RBS70, 40 on the torpedo.
  • Inherited defaultLaunch speed 250 and lifetime 1.35 appear on all five of APKWS, reduced APKWS, Hydra, reduced Hydra and Zuni. The five CB90 mortar shells store speed 0 and lifetime 30.
  • Our guessBecause so many types share one value, this looks like a base-class default. The inherited pair may not drive flight for these projectiles.

How sure Numbers: solid. Meaning: unproven, and the guess above is only a guess.

Still unknown
  • Whether the game uses these for anything.
  • The unit of both values.

Weapons by vehicle

These tables list the stored fire settings for each vehicle weapon. They use internal names from the files. The settings are labelled with what the file calls them. We do not know their units.

Do not read these as stats. A fire-rate setting is not shown to be shots per minute. A magazines setting is not a count of spare magazines. A refill delay of 5 is not shown to be 5 seconds. A magazine of -1 has no known meaning.

How these settings were named L3019, L3020, L3021, L3022, L3025, L3026, L3027, L3028, L3029, L640

  • Stored game valueThe names come from a control: an infantry weapon (the M4A1) whose same settings we can check. Where a vehicle weapon has the same layout, we use the same names.
  • Inherited defaultThat borrowing is by layout only. A few weapons use a different layout and could not be named: the AAV7A1 SP guns, the Jet Ski and the MH-47.

How sure Names: reasonable where the layout matches. Meaning and units: unknown.

Seats and loadouts

Seat entries L3100, L3101, L3250, L3253, L3255, L3257

  • Game descriptionThe cards state seat counts: MBT 2, IFV 6, light transport 5, armored transport 4, anti-air 1, attack helicopter 2, transport helicopter 5, attack jet 1, fighter jet 1.
  • Stored game valueThe UH-60 stores five seat entries (numbered 0 to 4), matching the transport helicopter card. The CB90 stores two entry definitions; the second leads to five mortar guns.
  • Stored game valueIn total we mapped 36 vehicles and 92 seat or station definitions. 49 of those stations have no gun in the file.
  • Stored game valueThere are 166 loadout slot rows and 151 equipment options. For 79 options we can name the seat that owns them. 72 are unresolved.
  • Stored game valueThe 120 mm tank main gun is fixed and is not one of the loadout choices.

How sure Counts: solid. They are counts of entries in the files, not proof of usable seats or defaults.

Still unknown
  • Which options a vehicle starts with.
  • Which seats players can actually use.
  • Which vehicle each card belongs to.
  • Which loadout recipes apply to which vehicle.

Smoke, flares and supply

Vehicle countermeasures and lock-on L3037, L3041, L3304

  • Stored game valueThermal smoke for infantry fighting vehicles, active protection for tanks (the Abrams is the example), and flares for attack helicopters all exist as named equipment with exact links to their vehicles.
  • Stored game valueThe remote turrets store three reload fields, MaxAmmoCountInWeapon, MinAmmoCountInWeapon and ReloadType, with the values -1, -1 and 1.
  • EA patch notesEA notes mention thermal smoke active time, a smoke screen cooldown and that laser designators affect locking range. We could not match them to stored fields.

How sure The equipment exists. How long it lasts, how many charges it has and what it does to locks are all unknown.

Still unknown
  • Smoke duration, charges and cooldown.
  • What the three reload values mean.
  • How locking range and time are calculated.

Vehicle Supply Crate L224, L233

Game description

A pallet that resupplies ammunition for nearby friendly vehicles.

  • Stored game valueConstruction and pickup limits are separate stages. Stored settings: Auto Construct true, Time To Construct 0.6, Resupply Interval 5.0. The units are not known.

How sure Numbers: carried from an earlier check, not re-read in this round. Meaning: unproven.

Still unknown
  • What it gives a vehicle and how fast.
  • What stops pickup.

Named health values L234

  • Stored game valueThe game keeps named vehicle values for current health, max health, normalised health, armour current and max, damage state and a wreck flag. We have the names, not how they are used.
